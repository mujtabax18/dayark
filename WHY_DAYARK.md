# Why I built DayArk

I started DayArk about two years ago. It wasn't a business idea. It wasn't something I thought everyone would download. It was a project for myself.

Whenever I needed something in my daily life—a reminder, a place for expenses, a way to save a document, a diary, a habit tracker—I tried adding it. As AI-assisted development became available, I used it as another tool to help me build and learn. I kept the parts I needed, changed what annoyed me, and added more things whenever an idea came to mind.

That is why DayArk has so many different features. Some of them might seem unrelated. To me, they belong together because **they are parts of everyday life**.

The latest idea I care deeply about is **Life Archive**. Sometimes I forget people's names. I wanted to keep track of the people around me, our relationships, the things we did together and memories I don't want to lose. I want to look back and remember people, not just events on a calendar.

I showed DayArk to people around me, and it wasn't really something they wanted to use. I understand that. But it made me happy to build it, and I thought: perhaps somewhere there is one person who would find it helpful.

So I am opening the source.

Not because it is perfect. It isn't. The UI needs work, and I am not a designer. Some code could be much cleaner. There will be bugs and things that are slower than they should be. It is still something real that I use, and I want people to see both what Flutter can do and where this project can improve.

I want software that lets us make better use of the computers and phones we already own. I am not against paid apps or subscriptions. I just believe people should also have tools that are free and work locally, without always asking them to create an account or pay for every feature.

I would love to see a friendlier UI, more themes, faster screens, a local AI assistant, and local-network chat, file sharing and calling. I'd also love better notes, alarms, a password manager, PDF tools, media playback, and radio. They are ideas in my head, and I will work on them when I can. Maybe someone else will want to work on one of them too.

You can use DayArk without giving anything back. If it helps you, that is already enough.

If you *do* want to help, help however you can. A bug report, one improved screen, a tiny fix, testing, a suggestion, or a kind message all count. I believe humanity moves forward when we help one another.

I don't know if many people will ever find this repository. Maybe no one will read these words. Still, I am writing them here—because maybe someone will, someday.

Thanks for visiting. Thanks to anyone who uses it. And thank you to everyone who contributes.
